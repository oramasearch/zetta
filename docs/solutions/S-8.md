# S-8 Predictive Maintenance

S-8 estimates asset failure risk and time-to-failure, then turns eligible
results into governed CMMS work. It is runnable at all four catalogue levels.
`create_solution` composes M-2 asset resolution, M-1 failure classification,
M-5 censoring-aware survival, and optional M-7 sensor anomaly context over a
frozen snapshot. `run_solution` selects one declared M-5 horizon, protects the
permanent holdout, applies the maintenance policy, delivers work-order tasks,
records the ledger, observes guardrails, and feeds `get_roi_readout`.

## Data contract

The caller lands asset history and supplies these roles:

| Role | Required | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| entity | yes | Stable asset identity and hierarchy key |
| label | yes | Binary observed-failure indicator; `0` means censored, not healthy forever |
| duration | yes | Time from prediction cutoff to failure or censoring |
| time | yes | Prediction cutoff, sensor time, failure time, and maintenance chronology |
| feature_time | yes | Timestamp proving every sensor value was available at the cutoff |
| feature | yes | Condition, usage, and environment sensor values |
| asset class | yes | Approved policy and safety scope |
| owner | yes | Maintenance team or CMMS queue |
| eligibility | yes | Maintenance window, class approval, safety, capacity, and active-order evidence |

Every decision row must explicitly pass `maintenance_eligible`,
`approved_asset_class`, `maintenance_window_available`, and
`safe_to_service`. `safety_lockout`, `active_work_order`, and
`sensor_data_untrusted` suppress work. Missing eligibility evidence blocks the
task. The caller also supplies the snapshot boundary, M-5 output/horizon
binding, CMMS webhook or connector destination, fallback owner, and ROI
economics. Delivery credentials are write-only Run input.

## Levels

| Level | Native stages | Behavior added |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | M-2, M-1, M-5 | Failure risk, expected and median time-to-failure, protected holdout, and CMMS work orders |
| 2 | level 1 | Asset-class maintenance-window policy over the same native callbacks |
| 3 | level 2 + M-7 | Sensor anomaly context alongside survival risk |
| 4 | level 3 | Approval-gated scheduling under the bounded policy |

Every level uses registered runnable methodology callbacks. M-5 is the primary
decision source; M-1 is not substituted for its survival table.

## Defaults

The policy starts with `imminent`, `elevated`, and `monitor` risk floors of
0.65, 0.25, and 0.0. Imminent risk produces an A0 draft work order. Elevated
risk produces an A2 inspection work order for eligible approved asset classes.
An owner may select `scheduled_work_order`, which is A1 and waits for approval.
Monitor-tier assets receive `no_action`.

An asset is limited to one work order in a rolling seven-day window and 24
recorded work orders over the policy lifetime. The default monetary budget is
zero because the action creates CMMS work rather than authorizing maintenance
spend. The kill switch is off. These are editable starter settings, not
calibrated maintenance recommendations.

Each risk tier has an independent permanent 10% holdout. Remaining assets enter
the `work_order` treatment cell. The named outcome is `failure_avoided` over 30
days. The monitor records unsafe-work-order rate, false-work-order rate,
missed-failure rate, sensor PSI, maintenance-capacity utilization, and action
execution. An absent observation is not treated as healthy.

## Run it

1. Create a Project and land a partitioned asset-history table.
2. Call `get_solution` with `{"solution":"S-8"}` and select a level.
3. Call `create_solution` with the request shape in
   [executable-pipelines.md](executable-pipelines.md). Supply M-5 duration and
   event hints plus `foundation.decision_source` naming `M-5`, `survival table`,
   its `entity` and `hazard` columns, and a horizon emitted by the configured
   interval width.
4. Call `run_solution` with the returned graph and optional delivery token,
   then poll `get_run`.
5. Read the decision table, policy, experiment, monitor, action tasks, and
   ledger. Delivered payloads carry asset, class, owner, work-order type, SLA,
   failure risk, horizon, expected/median time-to-failure, maintenance window,
   and decision cell.
6. Record `failure_avoided` outcomes against ledger action IDs and call
   `get_roi_readout` after the outcome window closes.

Webhook, Salesforce, and HubSpot delivery use the shared action outbox,
idempotency, retry, acknowledgement, dead-letter, kill-switch, holdout, and
audit behavior. A CMMS can receive the governed webhook payload through that
same integration layer.

## Correctness checks

`create_solution` and `run_solution` execute the same composed checks. Each
condition below is a refusal:

1. M-5 must retain event-indicator `0` assets as censored observations, use a
   named duration, and stop person-period expansion after observed failure.
   M-5, M-1, and M-2 are mandatory; levels 3 and 4 also require M-7.
2. Sensor drift monitoring must name at least one sensor, use a baseline ending
   at or before the prediction cutoff, and set PSI at or below 0.2.
3. Maintenance history must truncate at failure and exclude later records. The
   post-failure blocklist is nonempty, and selected predictors may not include
   failure codes, completed repairs, replacement reasons, post-failure
   inspections, or maintenance outcomes.

The planted-effect journey builds and runs S-8 over a partitioned synthetic
asset table, excludes a future asset, retains censored assets, verifies held-out
assets receive no task, checks the CMMS payload, observes all six guardrails,
records centered outcomes with a 0.18 failure-avoidance effect, and asserts that
`get_roi_readout` recovers the effect inside its 95% confidence interval.

Boundary: `foundation.decision_source.horizon` is an optional positive number
used to select one real row per asset from M-5's multi-horizon survival table.
All other records use existing graph, policy, experiment, monitor, action,
ledger, and ROI schemas. A live lake proof follows deployment of this branch.
