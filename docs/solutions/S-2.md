# S-2 Lead & Opportunity Scoring

S-2 scores conversion propensity for leads and opportunities, explains the
score through the shared native tabular pipeline, and routes governed CRM work
to an eligible owner with a bounded SLA. It is runnable at all four catalogue
levels. `create_solution` builds M-2 entity resolution, M-1 conversion scoring,
M-3 segmentation, and optional M-6 worked-lead incrementality over a frozen
snapshot. `run_solution` applies the versioned routing policy, protects the
holdout, delivers tasks through the action outbox, records the audit ledger,
updates guardrails, and feeds `get_roi_readout`.

## Data contract

The caller lands CRM history and supplies these roles:

| Role | Required | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| entity | yes | Stable lead, contact, account, and opportunity hierarchy key |
| label | yes | Conversion observed over a fully closed outcome window |
| time | yes | Score cutoff, CRM observation time, and conversion window |
| feature | yes | Pre-qualification engagement and firmographic inputs |
| owner | yes | Rep or queue eligible to receive the task |
| territory | yes | Routing territory and its current eligibility |
| capacity | yes | Rep capacity available at the decision cutoff |
| treatment | levels 3–4 | Worked, routed, nurtured, and no-contact assignment |
| value | level 4 | Opportunity value used by the approved routing policy |

Every action row must explicitly pass `eligible`, `territory_eligible`, and
`rep_capacity_available`. `excluded` and `do_not_contact` suppress delivery.
Missing eligibility evidence blocks the task. The caller also supplies the
snapshot boundary, dataset roles, CRM or webhook destination, fallback owner,
and ROI economics. Delivery credentials are write-only Run input.

## Levels

| Level | Native stages | Behavior added |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | M-2, M-1, M-3 | Conversion score, segments, protected holdout, capacity-aware queues, and SLA tasks |
| 2 | level 1 | Opportunity-risk configuration over the same native callbacks |
| 3 | level 2 + M-6 | Worked-lead incrementality from randomized treatment history |
| 4 | level 3 | Value-aware routing evidence under the approved policy |

All four levels compose only registered runnable methodology callbacks. Levels
3 and 4 attach M-6; no level substitutes an unrelated score table.

## Defaults

The policy starts with a `hot` score floor of 0.7 and a `standard` floor of
0.0. Hot leads create a `sales_queue_task` with a one-hour SLA. Standard leads
create a `nurture_queue_task` with a four-hour SLA. Both actions are A2. A hot
lead may be changed to `priority_queue_review`, which is A1 and therefore waits
for approval. `no_contact` remains an explicit alternative.

The lifetime contact cap is six. Each action is limited to one in a rolling
seven-day window. The default spend budget is zero because the shipped actions
are tasks, not offers. The kill switch is off. These are editable starter
settings, not calibrated business recommendations.

The experiment assigns each entity deterministically to a permanent 10%
holdout or the `routed` treatment cell. The named outcome is `converted` over
30 days. Guardrails require zero SLA misses, zero capacity violations, at least
0.9 routing coverage, and at least 0.95 action execution. Every metric is
observed by the S-2 Run rather than treated as healthy when absent.

## Run it

1. Create a Project and land a CRM history table.
2. Call `get_solution` with `{"solution":"S-2"}` and select a level.
3. Call `create_solution` with the request shape in
   [executable-pipelines.md](executable-pipelines.md). Supply M-3 role hints and
   the snapshot, dataset, delivery, and ROI settings. Optional verification
   overrides belong in `sources.correctness`.
4. Call `run_solution` with the returned graph and optional delivery token,
   then poll `get_run`.
5. Read the decision table, policy, experiment, monitor, action tasks, and
   ledger. The task payload includes routing queue, territory, queue owner,
   capacity evidence, decision cell, and the action SLA.
6. Record `converted` outcomes against ledger action IDs and call
   `get_roi_readout` after the outcome window closes.

Webhook, Salesforce, and HubSpot delivery use the shared idempotency, retry,
acknowledgement, dead-letter, kill-switch, holdout, and audit behavior.

## Correctness checks

`create_solution` and `run_solution` execute the same composed correctness
checks. Each condition below is a refusal:

1. Every methodology source must be a snapshot at or before scoring time. The
   named conversion label must be fully observed and its window must begin
   after the feature cutoff and blackout gap.
2. The post-qualification blocklist must be nonempty, and selected predictors
   must not include fields such as qualification status, opportunity stage,
   closed-won state, conversion outcome, or contract value.
3. Routing must name owner, territory, and current capacity columns, declare
   capacity-aware selection, and suppress over-capacity tasks. M-2 and M-3 are
   mandatory; levels 3 and 4 also require M-6.

The planted-effect journey builds and runs S-2 over a partitioned synthetic
CRM table, verifies held-out leads receive no task, checks owner/SLA/capacity
payloads, records centered conversion outcomes with a 0.15 routing effect, and
asserts that `get_roi_readout` recovers that effect inside its 95% confidence
interval.

Boundary: this module adds no new JSON format. It binds S-2 settings into the
existing graph attributes, policy, experiment, monitor, action, ledger, and ROI
schemas. A live lake proof follows deployment of this branch.
