# S-4 Collections Prioritization

S-4 prioritizes receivables for governed digital treatments and dialer queues.
It predicts point-in-time recovery propensity, resolves the actionable account,
groups comparable receivables, applies collections-regulation eligibility and
contact limits, protects a permanent holdout, delivers allowed work through the
action outbox, and measures incremental net recovery.

`create_solution` builds a runnable M-2 → M-1 → M-3 Pipeline at levels 1 and 2.
Levels 3 and 4 attach M-6 uplift selection. The M-1 stage publishes the real
`Prioritize collections treatments` output in
`collections_results.recovery_priority_<graph>`; the solution's default
`decision_source` binds that output's `receivable` and `recovery_score` columns
rather than inventing a score table.

## Data contract

The caller lands a receivables table and supplies these roles:

| Role | Required | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| entity | yes | Stable receivable, account, customer, or household key |
| label | yes | Promise-to-pay label observed at a known cutoff; net recovery is the measured outcome |
| time | yes | Promise observation time, delinquency cutoff, and contact-history cutoff |
| feature | yes | Balance, aging, payment, delinquency, and contact signals available at decision time |
| eligibility | yes | Explicit `collections_eligible`, `regulatory_contact_allowed`, and `within_contact_hours` evidence |
| vulnerability | yes | Explicit `vulnerable` evidence; true or missing evidence blocks contact |
| contact | yes | Prior contact counts and timestamps used by lifetime and rolling caps |
| treatment | level 3 | Randomized channel or offer plus its observed net-recovery outcome |
| owner | for routed work | Collections team or queue owner; otherwise the configured delivery owner is used |

The snapshot `time_column` is the promise-to-pay observation time. A later
status update is excluded by the inclusive snapshot cutoff. Every actionable
row must explicitly pass all eligibility predicates. Missing evidence is not
filled with an allowed value.

## Levels

| Level | Native stages | Behavior added |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | M-2, M-1, M-3 | Recovery prioritization, compliant policy, permanent holdout, digital treatment and dialer delivery, ledger, monitor, and readout |
| 2 | level 1 | Editable treatment tiers and champion/challenger operating policy over the same native stages |
| 3 | level 2 + M-6 | Positive-uplift selection from randomized treatment history |
| 4 | level 3 | Bounded digital treatment under the same eligibility, approval, contact-cap, and kill-switch controls |

Every level composes through the registered methodology callbacks. Levels 3
and 4 require the M-6 treatment and outcome roles in `sources.M-6`.

## Defaults

The policy has three tiers. Scores at or above 0.7 select `dialer_queue`;
scores at or above 0.4 select `digital_treatment`; lower scores select
`no_contact`. Digital and dialer work are A2 with a one-day SLA. A requested
settlement is A1 and requires approval. The starter spending budget is zero, so
the module cannot commit settlement value until an owner creates a later policy
version.

The lifetime contact cap is six. The rolling cap is one contact per seven days.
`collections_eligible`, `regulatory_contact_allowed`, and
`within_contact_hours` must be true, while `vulnerable` must be false. Each
predicate appears in both the allow and suppression rules so missing evidence
blocks delivery. The kill switch starts off.

The champion/challenger experiment assigns a deterministic 10% holdout and
splits treatment between `dialer_queue` and `digital_treatment`. Assignment is
stable for the project and graph. Guardrails cover complaints, regulatory
contact violations, vulnerable-customer contacts, contact frequency, and
action execution rate. The measured outcome is `net_recovery` over 30 days.
These are editable starter settings, not calibrated thresholds or return
claims.

## Run it

1. Create a project and land the receivables table.
2. Call `get_solution` with `{"solution":"S-4"}` and select a level.
3. Call `create_solution` with the snapshot, dataset roles, delivery endpoint
   and owner, and caller-supplied ROI economics. Put M-3 and optional M-6 hints
   under `sources.M-3` and `sources.M-6`.
4. Call `run_solution` with the returned graph and an optional write-only
   delivery token, then poll `get_run`.
5. Read decisions from the parent Run output, policy versions with
   `get_policy`, protected cells with `get_experiment`, action tasks through
   the action-integration read-backs, and guardrails with `get_monitor`.
6. Record `net_recovery` against every ledger action after the outcome window,
   then call `get_roi_readout`.

Digital treatments and dialer tasks use the shared webhook, Salesforce, or
HubSpot connectors with outbox ownership, idempotency, retry,
acknowledgement, dead-letter handling, holdout suppression, and kill-switch
rechecks. The task and ledger evidence retain the selected action, tier, owner,
SLA, decision, cell, and policy version.

## Correctness checks

`create_solution` and `run_solution` execute the same composed correctness
gate. Each condition below is a refusal:

1. The policy must enforce collections eligibility, permitted contact hours,
   a positive lifetime cap, and at most one contact in the rolling window.
2. Vulnerable customers must be excluded, and missing vulnerability evidence
   must block contact.
3. Promise-to-pay labels must name their observation-time column, use a frozen
   snapshot at or before prediction time, and exclude later status updates.

The shared snapshot, feature-lineage, methodology, and causal checks still
apply. Tests mutate each S-4 condition and verify its named refusal. The
synthetic journey runs build → run → decide → act → record outcomes → readout,
proves both action patterns use the action outbox, and recovers the planted
0.15 net-recovery effect inside its 95% confidence interval.

## Boundaries and later proof

This module adds no JSON or wire format. It configures existing solution,
policy, experiment, action, ledger, monitor, and ROI records. Snapshot cutoffs,
destinations, credentials, economics, and customer-specific policy calibration
remain caller supplied. The live lake proof is performed after deployment; it
is not a local test artifact.
