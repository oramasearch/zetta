# S-9 Automated Outbound

S-9 selects customer audiences, applies consent and suppression policy, and
triggers outbound journeys within lifetime and rolling contact caps. It is
runnable at all four catalogue levels. `create_solution` composes M-2 identity,
M-1 response propensity, M-3 audiences, and the selected M-6, M-8, and M-10
attachments over one frozen snapshot. `run_solution` protects a permanent
holdout, delivers allowed journeys through the action outbox, records every
contact and no-contact decision, observes guardrails, and feeds
`get_roi_readout`.

No-contact is a first-class result. Missing consent, active suppression,
channel ineligibility, an exhausted cap, a protected holdout, or non-positive
uplift prevents delivery and remains in the ledger for measurement.

## Data contract

The caller lands customer, engagement, treatment, and contact history and
supplies these roles:

| Role | Required | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| entity | yes | Stable resolved customer or account key |
| label | yes | Response observed over a fully closed outcome window |
| time | yes | Feature, eligibility, contact-history, and response cutoffs |
| feature | yes | Point-in-time engagement and audience attributes |
| consent | yes | Current consent for the selected channel |
| suppression | yes | Suppression, unsubscribe, and do-not-contact state |
| contact | yes | Lifetime and rolling-window contact counts |
| channel | yes | Eligible channel and journey-routing evidence |
| treatment | levels 2–4 | Journey, channel, and no-contact assignment with response |

Every decision row must explicitly provide `consent`, `suppressed`, and
`channel_eligible`. `unsubscribed` and `do_not_contact` suppress delivery.
`contacts` and `recent_actions` are compared with persisted policy decisions,
so a caller cannot erase prior contacts by supplying a lower count. The caller
also supplies the snapshot boundary, dataset roles, journey endpoint, owner,
and ROI economics. Delivery credentials remain write-only Run input.

## Levels

| Level | Native stages | Behavior added |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | M-2, M-1, M-3 | Response propensity, audience segments, capped journeys, permanent holdout, and response readout |
| 2 | level 1 + M-6 | Positive-uplift audience selection from randomized send/no-contact history |
| 3 | level 2 + M-8 | Eligible journey and channel arbitration under contact and budget constraints |
| 4 | level 3 + M-10 | Governed grounded assistance for approved outbound copy |

Every level calls registered native methodology callbacks. S-9's primary M-1
stage publishes `outbound response scores` with `entity` and
`response_propensity`; the module does not bind the churn result table. At
level 2 and above, M-6 positive uplift overrides propensity-only targeting.

## Defaults

The policy starts with priority, standard, and low score floors of 0.7, 0.4,
and 0.0. Priority entities select `outbound_journey`; standard entities select
`nurture_journey`; low entities select `no_contact`. Journey triggers are A2.
`copy_review` is an allowed A1 alternative and waits for approval. The default
monetary budget is zero.

The lifetime contact cap is four. An outbound journey is limited to one action
in seven days; nurture is limited to one in fourteen days. Eligibility requires
explicit consent, no suppression, and channel eligibility. Suppressed,
unsubscribed, and do-not-contact entities are excluded. These are editable
starter settings, not customer-specific calibration or business advice.

Each score tier receives an independent permanent 10% holdout probability; the
remaining population enters `journey_sent`. The named outcome is `response`
over 30 days. Guardrails cover unsubscribe rate, complaint rate, consent
violations, contact frequency, and action execution. The Run observes all five
metrics rather than treating absent evidence as healthy.

## Run it

1. Create a Project and land the outbound customer table.
2. Call `get_solution` with `{"solution":"S-9"}` and select a level.
3. Call `create_solution` with the request shape in
   [executable-pipelines.md](executable-pipelines.md). Supply M-3 roles and,
   from level 2, M-6 treatment/outcome roles. Optional verification overrides
   belong in `sources.correctness`.
4. Call `run_solution` with the returned graph and optional delivery token,
   then poll `get_run`.
5. Read the decision table, policy, experiment, monitor, action tasks, and
   ledger. Delivered payloads carry journey, channel, consent/suppression
   evidence, contact counts, SLA, and the audience decision cell.
6. Record `response` outcomes against ledger action IDs and call
   `get_roi_readout` after the outcome window closes.

Webhook, Salesforce, and HubSpot delivery use the shared idempotency, retry,
acknowledgement, dead-letter, kill-switch, holdout, and audit behavior.

## Correctness checks

`create_solution` and `run_solution` execute the same composed correctness
checks. Each condition below is a refusal:

1. `s9_consent_suppression` requires named current consent, suppression, and
   channel-eligibility evidence. Missing evidence and suppression must select
   no contact.
2. `s9_contact_caps` requires positive lifetime and rolling caps, named supplied
   count columns, persisted-decision counting, and no contact at either cap.
3. `s9_uplift_over_propensity` requires the outbound-specific M-1 output rather
   than the churn table. Every level requires M-2, M-1, and M-3; level 2 adds
   positive-only M-6 uplift, level 3 adds M-8, and level 4 adds M-10.

The planted-effect journey builds and runs level 1 over a partitioned synthetic
dataset, verifies consent, suppression, caps, and holdout protection, inspects
the outbound connector payload, records centered response outcomes with a 0.12
incremental effect, and asserts that `get_roi_readout` recovers the effect
inside its 95% confidence interval.

Boundary: S-9 adds no independent JSON format. It binds module settings into
the existing graph attributes, policy, experiment, monitor, action, ledger, and
ROI schemas. The generic M-1 runner now accepts additive result naming fields in
its existing coordinated-run options so outbound scores retain their meaning on
every participant. Live lake proof follows deployment of this branch.
