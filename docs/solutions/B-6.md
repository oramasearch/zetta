# B-6 Healthcare Operations

B-6 ranks assistive scheduling and revenue-cycle work without changing care
access or making clinical decisions. It is a runnable solution module:
`create_solution` builds its native M-1 Pipeline, and `run_solution` executes
the snapshot, score artifact, policy, protected holdout, action delivery,
ledger, monitor, and ROI readout.

The module retains its catalogue promotion condition: offer it to customers
only with a provider design customer that owns revenue-cycle operations and
has an agreed privacy posture. That commercial condition does not change its
engineering status: `get_solution B-6` reports `status: "catalogue"` and
`runnable: true`.

## Data contract

The caller lands one point-in-time operational row per appointment, claim, or
work case and supplies these roles:

| Role | Required | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| entity | yes | Stable appointment, claim, or operational-case key |
| label | yes | Observed attendance or claim-denial workflow outcome |
| time | yes | Scheduling or claim-state cutoff and outcome chronology |
| feature | yes | Approved minimum-necessary operational predictors |
| workflow | yes | Scheduling or revenue-cycle work type |
| protected group | yes | Evaluation-only equity cohort columns |
| privacy | yes | Recorded privacy approval and minimum-necessary review |
| owner | yes | Care-coordination or revenue-cycle work owner |

Protected-group columns are used for equity review and monitoring, not as
model features. Direct identifiers such as patient name, medical-record
number, address, phone, and email are blocked from the approved feature set.
The action payload carries the workflow type, decision identifier, tier,
assistive action, and minimum-necessary confirmation. It does not carry a
protected-group value or direct identifier.

## Levels

All four levels execute through the registered native M-1 callback. They share
the same healthcare-specific score artifact,
`healthcare_results.operation_risk_*`, with `entity` and `operational_risk`
columns.

| Level | Native stages | Behavior added |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | M-1 | Risk scores, protected holdout, and owned assistive tasks |
| 2 | M-1 | Scheduling and revenue-cycle work queues |
| 3 | M-1 | Protected-group operating review |
| 4 | M-1 | Bounded cross-workflow orchestration |

`get_solution.solution.levels` returns the four integer choices. The selected
level is passed to `create_solution.level` and remains recorded on the
composed Pipeline.

## Executable defaults

The policy starts with high/elevated/routine score floors of 0.7/0.4/0.0.
High risk defaults to `care_coordination_task`; elevated risk defaults to
`scheduling_assistance`; routine risk defaults to `no_action`. A source row may
request `revenue_cycle_review` where the selected tier allows it. All three
operational actions are A2. `assistance_exception_review` is A1 and requires
approval. `no_action` is A0.

Eligibility requires explicit operations eligibility, approved privacy
posture, minimum-necessary fields, a passed equity review, and evidence that
care access is unaffected. Patient opt-out, a clinical-decision request, a
care-denial request, or PHI beyond the approved scope blocks delivery. The
policy permits six lifetime actions and one action in a rolling seven-day
window. Its monetary budget is zero and its kill switch starts off.

Every risk tier has an independent permanent 10% holdout. Remaining entities
enter the `assistive_work` treatment cell. Measurement names the
`operational_resolution` outcome per case over 30 days. Guardrails record zero
care-denial actions, a maximum protected-group action-rate gap of 0.2, zero PHI
scope violations, zero privacy-posture failures, and a minimum action-execution
rate of 0.95. These are editable starter settings, not a clinical or financial
recommendation.

## Run it

1. Create a project and land the point-in-time operations table.
2. Call `get_solution` with `{"solution":"B-6"}` and select a level.
3. Call `create_solution` with the snapshot, dataset, delivery, and ROI inputs
   described in [executable-pipelines.md](executable-pipelines.md).
4. In `sources.correctness`, name the equity-review owner and protected-group
   columns; record the approved privacy review owner and identifier; and list
   the minimum-necessary feature names.
5. Call `run_solution`, then poll `get_run`. Read the real M-1 score table,
   protected experiment assignments, policy decisions, action ledger, and
   monitor.
6. Record `operational_resolution` outcomes against emitted ledger action IDs,
   then call `get_roi_readout` after the outcome window closes.

Webhook, Salesforce, and HubSpot delivery use the shared replicated outbox,
idempotency, acknowledgement, retry, dead-letter, kill-switch, and audit paths.
Protected holdout rows always record `no_contact` evidence and are never sent.

## Correctness checks

Creation and execution run the same composed correctness checks. Each condition
below is a refusal:

1. The equity review must be required, owned, cover at least one named
   protected-group column, review outcome rates, and state a valid maximum gap.
2. The policy must be assistive only, keep care access unchanged, exclude
   clinical decisions, and contain no tier or registered action that denies,
   cancels, restricts, or deprioritizes care.
3. The privacy posture must be recorded, approved, owned, identified by a
   review record, and required again by the runtime policy.
4. PHI minimization must be recorded. Every selected model feature must be in
   the approved minimum-necessary set and outside the direct-identifier
   blocklist.

Runtime policy validation repeats the care-access, privacy, equity, and
minimum-necessary requirements so a later policy edit cannot bypass the
created Pipeline. The synthetic journey creates and runs B-6, proves future
rows are excluded, verifies protected holdouts and assistive payloads, and
recovers a planted 0.18 operational-resolution effect inside its 95%
confidence interval.

## Boundaries and deferred work

No new standalone JSON or wire format is introduced. B-6 uses the existing
solution templates, composed-graph attributes, replicated policy and experiment
records, action tasks, ledger entries, monitor records, and ROI definition.
The external provider systems remain connector boundaries and are substituted
in tests.

Live deployed-Zetta verification is performed after this change is merged and
deployed; the local registry and journey exercise the same `get_solution`
capability and assert `runnable: true`.
